## NoMercy Torrent Downloader — v0.3.16

Enough peers to download from, and a browser that stops collecting tabs.

### Where peers come from

A tracker hands out fifty addresses and most of them are stale. Every other
client makes up the difference from the DHT and from the peers it is already
talking to — and this one asked neither. `Dht`, `PeerSearch`, `DhtStore`, `Pex`,
`PeerExchange`, `LsdSocket` and `LocalDiscovery` were all written, all
unit-tested, and none of them was ever constructed by the plugin. It advertised
the DHT bit in its handshake regardless.

That is why a swarm other clients see hundreds of seeds in gave this one a single
peer.

**Peer exchange.** The extension handshake asked only for `ut_metadata`, so no
peer ever offered one: a peer sends only what it was told this client speaks.
`ut_pex` is asked for now, and what a peer offers is dialled — during the metadata
fetch, where this client has fewest peers and needs them most, and while
downloading afterwards.

**The DHT.** `IDhtTransport` had no implementation, so there was nothing to run
the DHT over. There is now: one socket for every question, and one reading loop
that hands each answer to whoever is waiting on that transaction id, because UDP
carries no connection and reading whatever arrives next is how one node's answer
comes to be read as another's. The client joins the network at startup from the
two published nodes and asks it on every announce pass.

Two rules it keeps. Nothing is asked of the DHT until the metadata has arrived,
because whether a torrent is private is written in its info dictionary and BEP 27
says a private one is never looked for anywhere but its own tracker. And it
searches without announcing: an announce puts this client on the DHT's own list
of who has the file.

**The local network**, which neither a tracker nor the DHT can see. Announced
every five minutes, private torrents excepted — that packet carries the info hash
in the clear to everybody on the network.

### Ninety Chrome processes

The solver closed the tab it solved a challenge in, and left open every tab it
read a page in and every tab it posted a form in. A solve happens once per host;
a page is read on every name of every cycle. Ninety Chrome processes were found
on the owner's machine holding seven hundred megabytes, with nothing running.

Both paths close their tab now, however they return. The browser itself still
stays up between solves — that is what keeps a gated source's clearance, and it
is what made a slow leak a visible one.

### A block nobody asked for

`RequestLedger` was written to say what one peer may send, and nothing used it, so
a session took any block for any piece it was building, from any peer, and counted
it as downloaded. One ledger per peer now, written before the request is sent.

### Everything else

Unchanged from 0.3.15.
