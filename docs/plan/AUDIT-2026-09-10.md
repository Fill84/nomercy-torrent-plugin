# What the full audit of 10 September 2026 found

The owner asked for the whole chain to be walked end to end and measured rather than read, after
reporting that nothing on the Downloads page moves and that the search chain has never worked the
way `docs/00-goal.md` describes it.

Every finding below was **measured**. Where a measurement contradicts a document, the measurement is
recorded here and the document is corrected by the slice that fixes it.

## How it was measured

| Instrument | What it did |
| --- | --- |
| `tools/SourceHealth` | Walked all fourteen enabled catalogue entries against the real sites, three times, asking `South Park S15E12`. |
| A throwaway gate probe | Put the real `BrowserSolver` and a real tab on 1337x and EZTV and wrote down what the tab held every five seconds. |
| A throwaway tracker probe | Ran the real catalogue, readers, merge and cycle over the captured pages of four episodes and reported what reached the torrent client. |

Neither throwaway instrument is shipped. What they proved is turned into real tests by the slices
below.

## Finding 1 — the specification miscounts its own catalogue, and one word hides two things

`docs/05-sources.md` opens with two different numbers, three lines apart:

- line 3: *"Seventeen sources ship in `src/.../sources.json`"*
- line 5: *"All fifteen were measured working end to end on 13 August 2026"*

`sources.json` holds **fifteen** entries. Neither sentence is right about what they are, because
"source" is used for two things the code keeps strictly apart:

| What it is | Entries | Which |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| **Name sources** — where release information comes from | 5 entries, 4 sites | PreDB (feed + names), srrDB (feed), srrDB search (names), SceneSource (feed + names), EZTV latest (feed) |
| **Indexers** — the search pages asked who is serving a release | 10 entries, 9 asked | The Pirate Bay, 1337x, LimeTorrents, TorrentBay, EZTV, TorrentGalaxy, Torrentz2, TorrentDownloads, Nyaa; YTS ships `enabled: false` because films are out of scope |

`docs/00-goal.md` repeats the blur: *"The fifteen public sources ship with the plugin"*.

**Why it matters, and it is not tidiness.** `SourceRole` exists precisely because the two answer
different questions, and known failure **A2** is what happened when they were confused: an RSS feed
was put in the search set and asked a question per episode — forty identical requests a cycle. The
word that flattens the distinction is still the first word of the document that is supposed to be the
specification.

### Spec

1. `docs/05-sources.md` states one count, and it is the count in `sources.json`. It says how many are
   name sources and how many are indexers, and it never calls an indexer a source in prose.
2. `docs/00-goal.md` says the same numbers with the same two words.
3. The catalogue table in `docs/05-sources.md` is the list of record; the counts in prose are derived
   from it and stated once.
4. A test holds the document against the file, so neither can drift again without something going
   red.

### Steps

1. Test (red): parse the catalogue table out of `docs/05-sources.md`, parse `sources.json`, and
   assert the two hold the same names, the same kinds and the same enabled flags, and that every
   count named in the prose of both documents equals what the file holds. It fails today on
   "seventeen".
2. Correct the two documents. Nothing else.

**Done when** that test passes, the whole suite is green, and neither document names a number that is
not in `sources.json`.

## Finding 2 — the plugin throws away the page it just waited for

**Two of the nine indexers have answered nothing at all, for at least ten days, and nothing said so.**
1337x carries priority 40, the second highest in the catalogue; EZTV is the television specialist.

### What was measured

`tools/SourceHealth`, three runs, same term:

| Run | Browser profile | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | as found | **12 of 14** — 1337x and EZTV "did not answer" |
| 2 | after the probe had solved a challenge | **14 of 14** — 1337x read 9 rows |
| 3 | wiped, cold | **12 of 14** — both silent again |

Then a tab was watched directly, cold:

```
01:36:47   28.672 chars, loaded=True, challenge=True, title='Just a moment...'
01:36:52  653.920 chars, loaded=True, challenge=True, title='Download South Park S15E12 Torrents | 1337x'
          cf_clearance present
```

**The challenge cleared in five seconds.** The real search page was in the tab, 654 KB, nine torrent
rows, and the clearance cookie was earned. `BrowserSolver.WaitForClear` polls
`!CloudflareChallenge.IsChallengePage(body) && IsLoadedAsync`, and `IsChallengePage` kept answering
**true** on that page until the forty-five seconds ran out, at which point the fetch reported "the
browser could not get past it".

### Root cause

`CloudflareChallenge.Markers` lists `challenge-platform`. On the cleared page Cloudflare leaves this
behind:

```js
a.src='/cdn-cgi/challenge-platform/scripts/jsd/main.js'
```

That is Cloudflare's JavaScript-detections script. **It rides on ordinary successful responses.** It
is not a challenge, and the marker matches it.

Counted on the real pages:

| Page | `cf_chl_opt` | `Just a moment` | `challenge-platform` | `cf-browser-verification` |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| the interstitial, 1337x | 7 | 1 | 1 | 0 |
| the interstitial, EZTV | 7 | 1 | 1 | 0 |
| the cleared page, challenge just solved | 0 | 0 | **1** | 0 |
| the cleared page, clearance already held | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |

So `cf_chl_opt` and `Just a moment` identify the interstitial and appear nowhere else;
`challenge-platform` is the only marker that cannot tell the two apart, and it is the one that
decides. `cf-browser-verification` is a legacy marker that no page served today carries.

**Why it was invisible.** The class doc says it: *"These are not pinned by a captured page yet — there
is no capture of a challenge in `tests/fixtures/`."* A detector with no capture to test against is a
detector nobody can prove wrong. It also explains why it worked in 0.3.15 and stopped: with a
clearance already in the profile Cloudflare serves the page without the detections script, so the
fault only shows the first time — and then hides itself for as long as the clearance lasts.

### Spec

1. A page is a challenge when it says it is one. In a browser that means `cf_chl_opt` or a
   `Just a moment` title. `cf-browser-verification` is kept because an older Cloudflare serves it and
   it costs nothing.
2. `challenge-platform` is not a marker. It appears on pages that are not challenges, and treating it
   as one rejects the answer.
3. Over HTTP the `cf-mitigated` header stays authoritative and unchanged; it is a statement about the
   response rather than a guess about markup.
4. Both answers — the response one and the browser one — are pinned by real captured pages, so the
   two can never disagree about what a challenge is.

### Steps

1. Capture, into `tests/fixtures/`: the real interstitial and the real cleared page that carries the
   detections script. Both are already taken.
2. Test (red): the cleared capture through `IsChallengePage` is **false**. It is true today.
3. Test (must stay green): the interstitial capture through `IsChallengePage` is true, and the same
   body with a `403` and a `cf-mitigated` header through `IsChallenge` is true.
4. Drop `challenge-platform` from `Markers`. Say in the remark why, and name the script.
5. Re-run `tools/SourceHealth` from a **cold profile** and record the result here. It must read 1337x
   and EZTV without the profile having to be warm.

**Done when** those tests pass, the whole suite is green, and a cold-profile health run reads
fourteen of fourteen.

## Finding 3 — not one tracker reaches the torrent client

`docs/00-goal.md` step 5 says the matches are merged *"so one torrent carries every tracker"*.
`docs/05-sources.md` says *"Merge by info hash, union the trackers"*. Measured over four episodes and
four sites, through the real catalogue, readers, merge and cycle:

```
### silo8  (Silo S03E08):   37 torrents after merging — with a magnet: 0; with any tracker: 0
### silo4  (Silo S04E01):   34 torrents after merging — with a magnet: 0; with any tracker: 0
### sugar1 (Sugar S02E01): 109 torrents after merging — with a magnet: 0; with any tracker: 0
### sugar  (Sugar S02E08):                            — with a magnet: 0; with any tracker: 0
```

And what the client is handed for the release the cycle chooses:

```
TAKEN  magnet:?xt=urn:btih:971888DF45B263EEA92DF8BF0DDBA1E74938983E&dn=Sugar%202024%20S02E08%20...
TAKEN  trackers=0
```

A bare magnet. No `&tr=` at all. The swarm can then only be found through the DHT, which is why
torrents sit at "fetching metadata" with no peer and no seed.

### Root cause

Three facts, each measured:

1. **No listing publishes a magnet.** Counted across every capture: `sugar-*`, `silo4-*`, `silo8-*`,
   `lucky2-*`, `the-pirate-bay.json`, `nyaa.xml` — zero magnets, zero `tr=` parameters. The code
   already knows: `Magnets.cs` says *"No shipped indexer publishes a magnet on its listing — not one
   of the nine captured"*.
2. **The trackers are on the row's own page.** Those captures carry them:
   `limetorrents-detail.html` 20, `x1337-detail.html` 21, `torrentdownloads-detail.html` 15,
   `eztv-detail.html` 7.
3. **`Find.FollowAsync` never reads that page when the row already carries an info hash.** It returns
   `Magnets.For(hash, title)` instead, which builds `magnet:?xt=urn:btih:{hash}&dn={title}` — a magnet
   with no tracker in it — and the page is never fetched.

So `copy.Trackers` is empty, `TrackerBook.Learn` is fed nothing, `DefaultTrackers` stays empty for
ever, and every grab goes out trackerless. `SearchCycle.cs` states the contradiction in a comment of
its own: *"No shipped listing publishes a magnet, so a torrent's trackers are not known until its own
page has been read."*

**Why no test caught it.** `FindTests` does assert a tracker union — on hand-built `ReleaseCopy`
objects carrying `udp://one.example:80`, an address that exists nowhere. No test looks at what the
torrent client is handed. That is **H1** exactly: the test covers the fault instead of finding it.

### Spec

The owner's rule, 10 September 2026: **an indexer exists only to obtain a torrent or a magnet. Every
tracker comes from the torrent or the magnet itself. Every indexer carrying the same info hash is
merged into one magnet with all the trackers of all of them.**

1. **One pass, then decide.** Every indexer is asked the correct release name once per episode, as it
   is now. Nothing is asked twice and nothing is asked again per download.
2. **A merged torrent remembers where it came from.** Merging by info hash keeps every contributing
   row's route to the artefact — its own page, its claim, its magnet — one per indexer.
3. **The release that is taken is resolved across every indexer that has it**, once: each route is
   read for its magnet or torrent, and every tracker of every one of them is unioned onto the one
   magnet handed to the client. At most one request per indexer per download, and none per row.
4. **A hash is a fallback, never a reason not to look.** Where a route answers nothing, the magnet
   built from the hash is still handed over, so The Pirate Bay — which publishes a hash and no page at
   all — keeps working exactly as it does now.
5. What is learned this way feeds `TrackerBook` as it already does, so the list grows with use.
6. A private tracker's announce address is never learned. `TrackerBook`'s existing rule stands
   untouched.

### Steps

1. Capture, into `tests/fixtures/`, the row pages of one release from every indexer that carries it,
   so the union can be asserted against real pages rather than invented ones.
2. Test (red): the four captured listings plus those row pages, through the real chain; the
   `TorrentRequest` the client is handed carries the union of the trackers of every one of those
   pages, and it is not empty. It is empty today.
3. Test (red): the number of fetches for row pages equals the number of indexers carrying the chosen
   hash, never the number of rows.
4. Test (must stay green): a copy whose only source publishes no page at all is still handed over with
   the magnet built from its hash.
5. `ReleaseCopy` keeps the routes of the rows merged into it.
6. `Find.FollowAsync` becomes a resolve across all routes, and loses the early return on a known hash.
   The reason for that early return — The Pirate Bay's rows being unreachable while the page was tried
   first — is kept by step 4's fallback rather than by not looking.
7. Rewrite the merge tests that assert unions of invented trackers against the captures. A test that
   cannot fail for a real reason is worse than none.

**Done when** those tests pass, the whole suite is green, and a real download on `beast-unit` is seen
to start with a tracker list rather than an empty one.

## Finding 4 — one release commonly has two info hashes, and the second is usually nearly dead

Not a fault; a measured fact the next slice needs. Across the four capture sets most releases appear
under two different info hashes, and the two are rarely comparable:

| Release | Hash A | Hash B |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| `Silo S03E08 1080p HEVC x265-MeGusta` | 5639 seeders | 71 |
| `Silo S03E08 Gray Goo 720p ATVP WEB-DL DDP5 1 Atmos H 264-playWEB` | 2445 | 25 |
| `Silo S03E04 1080p WEB H264-CAKES` | 1774 (The Pirate Bay) | 238 (LimeTorrents) |
| `Sugar 2024 S02E01 … 720p ATVP WEB-DL DDP5 1 H 264-NTb` | 57 (The Pirate Bay) | 10 (The Pirate Bay again) |

Two different info hashes are two different swarms and cannot become one download.

**The owner's decision, 10 September 2026: both are started at once and the first to finish is kept.**
That is a slice of its own, after Findings 2 and 3 are proved on the owner's own server, because it
reaches the client, the transfers tick, staging and the disk accounting — and those are the parts that
must not break.
